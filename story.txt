The lights in the old lighthouse had been dark for thirty years, so when Maya saw a beam sweep across the bay at midnight, she knew something was wrong. She grabbed her flashlight and ran down the rocky path, her heart pounding louder than the waves. At the top of the spiral stairs, the lamp room door stood wide open, and a single brass key sat glowing on the floor. Then, from somewhere below, she heard footsteps climbing toward her.

Maya gripped the brass key as a tall figure stepped into the lamp room. It was her grandfather, the man everyone in town believed had vanished at sea thirty years ago, his coat still dripping with seawater. "I'm sorry I took so long to come home," he whispered, but before Maya could answer, a young woman in a faded keeper's uniform appeared behind him. "The others still need the light," the woman said, pointing to a fleet of fishing boats emerging from the mist, with the smallest drifting toward the rocks.

Together, Maya and her grandfather fitted the brass key into the rusted control box beneath the lamp. Maya turned it to send three warning flashes across the water, and the small boat changed course just in time. Her grandfather steadied the beam toward the harbor, guiding the rest of the lost fleet safely home. When they turned to thank the keeper, she had disappeared. On the wall, Maya spotted a photograph dated thirty years earlier showing the same young woman beside the lamp. Her grandfather touched the frame. "She promised she would bring us back," he said softly.

At sunrise, after the last fishing boat reached the harbor, Maya returned to the lighthouse with a toolbox and her grandfather at her side. Together, they would make sure its light never went dark again.
