The lights in the old lighthouse had been dark for thirty years, so when Maya saw a beam sweep across the bay at midnight, she knew something was wrong. She grabbed her flashlight and ran down the rocky path, her heart pounding louder than the waves. At the top of the spiral stairs, the lamp room door stood wide open, and a single brass key sat glowing on the floor. Then, from somewhere below, she heard footsteps climbing toward her.

Maya snatched up the brass key as a young woman in a faded keeper's uniform stepped into the doorway. "The light is a warning," the stranger said, pointing to a small boat drifting toward the rocks below. Maya fitted the key into the rusted control box beneath the lamp and turned it, sending three bright flashes across the water. The boat changed course just before the waves could smash it against the shore, and Maya let out the breath she had been holding. When she turned to thank the woman, the room was empty except for a photograph dated thirty years earlier: the same woman stood beside the lamp, smiling. At sunrise, Maya returned with a toolbox, determined that the lighthouse would never go dark again.
